Case study 01 · Account leadership

Taking over and rebuilding the account

Ahead of targetHalfway through the contract, the account had already outgrown the whole previous contract year in views and clicks, and already hit several of its KPIs for the full term, including the follower targets on TikTok and LinkedIn and the engagement targets on Instagram and LinkedIn

Context

I took the account over a month into a new contract term, when the previous team left. The contract covers social media, email and strategy, and I was asked to reorganise the team and restructure how the account ran.

My role

I lead the account and the client relationship, report to the CEO, and direct a remote team across countries, including the on site team in Singapore.

What I did

  1. Set new standards for how the team works, from SOPs and briefs to how work is reviewed, so a remote team across countries delivers to the same bar, including a shared, responsible approach to AI (see case study 03)
  2. Cut review and revision rounds on eDMs by drafting copy and mock designs with AI, built on data about what has worked before, so the client picks a direction before a designer starts
  3. Built a shared content and budget dashboard, a web app made with AI assisted development, that replaced scattered spreadsheets and docs, so the client and our team see proposed content, quota and budget in one place
  4. Moved the monthly media report onto an AI assisted workflow, so the team spends less time compiling data and more time on what worked, what didn’t and what to change in content, budget and A/B tests
  5. Planned and rolled out two big changes for the client across social and email, on a set timeline: Skills and Training Advisory becoming Careers & Skills Advisory, with services expanded from course advice to career exploration, resume guidance and job search help, and the launch of Connect by SWDA, a new careers and skills centre inside the LLSG building, timed around construction, the Prime Minister’s opening, the festival and SWDA’s own announcements

Case study 02 · Campaign

SkillsFuture Festival 2026, on half the budget

9 weeksdrove more clicks to the festival site than the account drew in the whole previous contract year, 2025 festival included, on just over half the planned budget

Brief

A nine week publicity campaign for SkillsFuture Festival 2026: Career Health in the Age of AI, the annual SkillsFuture Festival, aimed largely at mid career Singaporeans. The festival opened with the Prime Minister launching Connect by SWDA, a new careers and skills centre, which the campaign also had to introduce to the public.

My role

I led the campaign strategy, creative direction and paid media, with the team at its largest across countries and on site in Singapore.

One key visual, many pieces

From my campaign report: twelve paid boosts timed around ten roadshows over two months, set against daily clicks and views. Click to enlarge.

What I did

  1. Turned the client’s single key visual into a full campaign look across four platforms and email
  2. Planned the content around the festival calendar: launch, ten district roadshows and the opening of Connect by SWDA, delivering every planned content area and expanding several
  3. Ran a festival content calendar of around 50 posts over nine weeks, posting almost every day in the busiest weeks: teasers and countdowns before launch, highlights from each roadshow within a day or two, and a round up of what’s on every Monday
  4. Built recurring series, including staff spotlights and weekly roundups, paired with matching video and carousels
  5. Directed an on site team in Singapore covering the roadshows, while running the account remotely
  6. Timed twelve paid boosts around the roadshows, tested by region and objective, and moved budget to what worked week by week
  7. Wrote the festival eDMs, with copy and layout decisions based on what had performed before

Case study 03 · Ways of working

AI in practice, people in charge

The agency’s leadership wanted AI built into how the team works, and I led that change on this account. LLSG’s work is helping Singaporeans adapt to a job market that AI is changing, so the way we use AI ourselves has to be responsible and set an example.

Why it matters

LLSG plays a key part in AI upskilling for Singapore’s workforce, but the people it most needs to reach, mid careerists aged 30 to 50, are not the easiest audience. Many find AI intimidating or don’t know where to start, and some worry it will take their jobs. Coming from a government backed account, how we talk about AI carries weight, so the approach has to be gentle, accurate and never dismissive of those fears. With no set playbook for this, the team has to understand AI well enough to explain it simply, and use it the way we tell others to, with real people only and never AI generated images of people. We hold ourselves to a high standard on keeping human judgement and creativity at the centre of everything we make.

What I set up

  1. Rolled AI in gradually, after learning how the account worked: team wide training for producers, a written SOP for every new process, and one on ones with each lead, such as agreeing with the design lead where AI fits and where it doesn’t
  2. Trained producers to prompt like creative directors, with references and a clear tone, so the work stays theirs and never looks like generic AI output. That answered the team’s worry that AI would take over their creative role
  3. Connected our AI tools to what we’ve learned: the files where the client leaves comments, our best performing posts and eDMs, and the current feed, so drafts build on what worked and stay on brand
  4. Set clear rules: real people only, never AI generated images of people; and every AI draft fact checked and edited by a person, with the telltale habits of AI writing stripped out
  5. Got the team to set up their own AI accounts to the same standard, with written instructions, shared skill files and organised project folders, so every producer starts from the same rules
